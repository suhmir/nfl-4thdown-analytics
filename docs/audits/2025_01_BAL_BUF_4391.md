## 2. How nfl4th values "go for it" (from source, commit 1aa982f)

```
go_wp = P(convert) × WP(succeed) + (1 − P(convert)) × WP(fail)
```

* **P(convert)** comes from `fd_tables` (R/go_model.R), tabulated by distance and field position and shifted by spread, total and era. A "late/desperate" boost applies only when the offense **trails** in the last 10 minutes or its win probability is below 15%. **Baltimore was leading at about 79%, so no late boost applies.**
* **WP(succeed)** and **WP(fail)** come from nfl4th's own first-down win-probability model (R/apply_win_prob.R). Its features include **both teams' timeouts**, and it has an explicit end-of-game rule: *"a team ahead that can kneel out the clock wins (WP 1)."* With Buffalo at 0 timeouts and about 1:27 left after a conversion, that rule should set **WP(succeed) ≈ 1.0**.
* **WP(fail)** is Buffalo with 1st & 10 near Baltimore's 38 (nfl4th uses a distribution of where failed attempts end, not just the line of scrimmage), about 1:27 left, 0 timeouts, trailing by 2, so a field goal wins.

| League 4th & 3 conversion rate (all situations) | 142 attempts | **50.7%** |

Down 2 instead of 3, a field goal *wins* rather than ties, so Buffalo's chance after a Baltimore failure should be **higher** than 56–60%. That puts Baltimore's WP(fail) at roughly **25–40%**.

## 4. Sensitivity (assuming WP(succeed) = 1.0)

| WP(fail) for BAL | P(convert) implied by nfl4th's 78.8% | Break-even P(convert) vs. punting (71.7%) |
|---|---|---|
| 20% | 73.5% | 64.6% |
| 25% | 71.8% | 62.2% |
| 30% | 69.8% | 59.5% |
| 35% | 67.4% | 56.4% |
| 40% | 64.7% | 52.8% |
| 45% | 61.5% | 48.5% |
| 50% | 57.7% | 43.3% |

Break-even formula: `P* = (WP_punt − WP_fail) / (1 − WP_fail)`.

**Reading it:** if Baltimore's chance after a failed attempt is in the 25–40% range suggested by the anchors, nfl4th's 78.8% only holds if it gives Baltimore a **65–72%** chance to convert 4th & 3, well above the league's 51%. If nfl4th's conversion probability is closer to the mid-50s, then its 78.8% implies it values the failure branch at about **50%** for Baltimore. That's generous for a defense facing Josh Allen at its own 38 with 1:27 left and a field goal winning the game.

**Either way, the 7.15-pt grade depends heavily on WP(fail).** Going beats punting as long as conversion exceeds roughly 53–62% across the plausible WP(fail) range. That's a real edge if nfl4th's numbers hold, but it is closer and more assumption-dependent than "+7.15, 100% of bootstrap draws" suggests. The bootstrap only resamples nfl4th's own conversion and first-down WP fits; it doesn't test whether this end-game state is valued correctly.
